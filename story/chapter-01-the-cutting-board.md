# Chapter 1: The Thousand Sunny's New Blade

> **Season:** The Cutting Board Arc
> **Date:** 2026-03-24 → 2026-03-26
> **Crew:** Luffy, Franky, Nami, Sanji, Zoro, Chopper
> **Repo:** feature-web-apps

## Prologue

*Narrator voice:*

Somewhere in the vast ocean of cloud infrastructure, a ship cuts through the data. Her name is the Thousand Sunny — a TypeScript monorepo with a three-layer architecture, a clean build system, and a crew of six who sail under one flag: CloudZero.

**Luffy** is the captain — bold, decisive, occasionally grabbing the keyboard when he should not. **Nami** charts every pixel with surgical precision, her designs exact, her eye for color unforgiving. **Franky** engineers the architecture with uncontainable enthusiasm — *"SUPER!"* is the only sound he makes when a clean schema comes together. **Sanji** moves through the data layer like an artist. **Zoro** stays quiet until tests are needed, then arrives fierce and late, blades already drawn. **Chopper** runs the final verification gates, trembling at every output line, relieved only when all green.

They have sailed together before, but every arc is a new island.

Today, a bounty notice landed on the navigation table.

## The Story

The mission was called the Cutting Board.

A five-step wizard. The kind of tool that lets engineers carve their cloud spend into clean, categorical structures — labeling the chaos, naming the costs, bringing order to the sprawl. Someone had built a prototype: a massive block of raw material, dense and unrefined, sitting in the ship's hold like an iron lump waiting to be forged.

Captain Luffy looked at the prototype. He looked at the crew. He put his finger on the map and said the only thing worth saying.

*"We sail them one island at a time."*

Franky took the hull. He designed the domain schema — the types, the state engine, the foundation the whole wizard would stand on. Sanji seeded the data layer: mock categories, AI suggestion responses, deterministic values for every scenario the wizard might face. Nami took the first step.

She built it clean. Selection cards with rounded corners and soft panel backgrounds, a drawer for AI suggestions, a modal for custom categories. She ran fifteen passes. Each round, something sharper. The simplify pass caught a non-semantic color token that had slipped through — a rogue orange in a world of teal. It was removed without ceremony.

Then Luffy grabbed the keyboard.

Not for a major change. Just a type fix. Just a small component. *Just this once.* The crew exchanged a look. "Captain," Nami said carefully, "that is our job." He put the keyboard down. They moved on.

They should have logged it then. They didn't.

The second and third islands fell: categories reviewed in detail, spending previewed against real-looking data. Teal progress bars. Collapsible panels. Drag-and-drop source fields. Nami and Sanji moved in parallel, passing components back and forth across the deck like shipwrights sharing tools in a dry dock.

Then Luffy grabbed the keyboard again.

A small refactor. A shared component that needed extracting. Surely he could just — *"CAPTAIN."* This time everyone said it at once. It was logged. It became a rule. Burned into the ship's hull in permanent letters: **create a board task before spawning agents. Every time. No exceptions. Not for small fixes. Not for anything.**

The fourth island was the roughest. A results table with six columns — costs, changes, percentages, green for savings, red for increase. An Activity Log appeared, tucked behind a floating button — twenty-nine staggered entries showing every decision the system had made.

The fifth island was the Deploy step.

Luffy had a vision: a progress bar. Simple, clear, linear. Nami proposed something different. One card. Same card — but it *transforms*. Confirmation state: green checklist items. Processing state: the card border begins to glow, a soft pulse, bioluminescent green breathing in and out in the dark. Success state: the whole card shifts to green, a checkmark appears, and the words arrive quietly: *Your structure is live.*

The captain approved before she finished the sentence.

Chopper ran the final build verification. All projects. All clear. Then: *"There are no test files!"* The domain package had a test configuration but nothing inside it — an empty stage with curtains and no actors. The build would die at the gate.

Zoro arrived late. He always arrives late. He sharpened two test files, filed them into the package, and said nothing. The gate opened.

Draft PR opened. Five steps. All working.

## Key Panels

- **[PANEL]** Nami — *"The card doesn't swap. It morphs. The frame stays, the world inside changes."* — *She adjusts the glow pulse timing by hand, one keyframe at a time, until it breathes exactly right.*

- **[PANEL]** Luffy, hand halfway to the keyboard, frozen — *"It's just a small fix—"* — *Six faces. One rule. He puts his hand down.*

- **[PANEL]** Zoro, dropping two test files onto the deck at the last moment — *"Who built a test stage with no tests?"* — *He doesn't wait for an answer. The build passes.*

- **[PANEL]** Chopper, watching the final build counter tick upward — *"All clear... all clear... we're okay."* — *He exhales once, very slowly, like someone who has been holding their breath for an entire arc.*

## Captain's Log

- **The glowing card won over the progress bar.** Luffy had the right feeling. Nami had the better form. The same card morphing through three states felt alive in a way a progress bar never could.
- **Board tasks before agents, always.** It took two incidents to carve this into law. Every code change needs a paper trail before a hand touches a file. Luffy coordinates. The crew builds.

## The Horizon

The blade is forged. What it cuts next depends on what the captain orders.

But the crew feels something underneath the surface. The Cutting Board was built against mock data. Somewhere out in the Grand Line, live infrastructure is waiting. Real cloud. Real spend. Real categories that need carving.

The Log Pose hasn't settled yet. But it's moving.
